\section{Research Questions}

Our goal is to determine the optimal passing strategy for a Formula 1 race
at a specific circuit. We will examine situations in which one driver is
trying to pass the car directly ahead and identify factors that may indicate
whether a pass will succeed.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.25em]
    \item Where on the circuit do passes most often occur?
    \item How do gap and closing speed relate to the likelihood of a pass?
    \item Do tire compound and tire age for the following and leading cars
          relate to pass probability?
    \item How does DRS use relate to pass probability in seasons when DRS
          data is available? (This may be out of scope.)
\end{enumerate}

\section{Techniques}
Various techniques will be used from both Volume 3 and Volume 4. Here are a few that we think will be most helpful.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.25em]
    \item Clustering: This will probably be the biggest method used as we will cluster attribute combinations into successful and unsuccessful pass attempts. 
    \item Regression: This will likely be used to normalize pass attempts based on weather inputs. 
    \item HMM's: Much of this data is time series data so using some sort of markov chain could be helpful. We are not sure exactly how we will do this but it could work well.
    
\end{enumerate}

\section{Metrics}
Measuring the probability of a pass will likely be relatively easy. A pass is either successful or not. To determine which attributes contribute most to a successful pass we will begin with a train/test split and compare our accuracy of our built model to our test case accuracy. We can do a K-fold split to improve this result. When doing our regression to normalize for weather we can use MAE and RMSE.
